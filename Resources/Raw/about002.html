Quick Start

  1. Pick your instrument.
  2. Choose a tune or scale.
  3. Tap Listen.
  4. Play the notes in order.
  5. Watch for green boxes.

Tip: If a list is long, flick to scroll quickly.

Welcome to Music Mate

Music Mate helps you practice your instrument one note at a time. It listens while you play and gives you feedback right away. When you play the correct note, you will see it on the screen. As you keep practicing, Music Mate remembers how you are doing and helps you work on notes that still need practice.

You can explore the app by tapping around. You cannot break anything. If you ever want to start over, use the Reset button to return to the starting settings.

Getting Around

Tap the menu button (☰) at the top left to open the different pages in the app.

  • Home – where you practice
  • Settings – where you choose how the app works
  • Statistics – where you can see your progress
  • Advanced – extra settings for adults, teachers, or experienced users
  • About – the page you are reading now

To move up or down on a page, drag to scroll. Some long lists move faster if you flick them quickly.

Home Page

Notes on the Staff

Music Mate shows notes on a musical staff with a treble clef. In the usual starting setup, you will see a short group of notes going up and down over one octave.

Feedback

  • When you play the correct note, the box turns green.
  • If the note is not correct, the box stays the same.
  • The app keeps track of how you are doing as you practice.

Sometimes Music Mate also shows how far your note is from the correct pitch in cents. A cent is a very small pitch difference. 100 cents = 1 semitone.

Main Controls

Instrument

Choose the instrument you play. Music Mate remembers your choice for next time.

Key

Choose the key signature you want to practice in. For transposing instruments, the app shows the music in the right key for your instrument.

Concert Key

If needed, the concert key is also shown.

Major

The Major scale is the default value of the many practice modes in this picker.

Start / Stop Listening

  • Tap the green round button to make the phone start to listen to your play.
  • When listening, the button changes to a red square.
  • Tap the red square to stop listening.

Auto Play

Tap the green triangle button to make the phone play the notes. This can help you learn how a scale or exercise should sound. While the notes are playing, the button turns red.

Background Color

You can change the background color of the staff area. Choose a color you like, then tap OK. Other pages use the chosen color for their background.

Repeat New

Toggle to green to make it automatically generate a new random pattern after a few seconds.

Repeat

Toggle to green to make it start listening from the beginning after a few seconds.

Practice Modes

Tuner

In Tuner mode, Music Mate listens to any note you play and displays:

  • the nearest note name
  • the octave number
  • how sharp or flat the note is
  • the note’s frequency and the nearest standard frequency

This is useful for checking tuning and pitch.

Random

In Random mode, Music Mate makes a note pattern for you to play using:

  • the note range you choose
  • note jumps that are common in melodies
  • the accidental percentage setting
  • your past results, so it can focus on notes that still need practice

As you improve, notes you already know well can be left out, so you spend more time practicing notes that need more work. You can also turn on Repeat New so a new random pattern appears automatically after you finish one. Or Repeat Same will make it repeat the same pattern of notes after a few seconds.

Scales

When you choose a scale, Music Mate shows the key signature, one octave going up, and the notes coming back down. This helps you practice reading, hearing, and playing scales.

Settings Page

On the Settings page, you can change how Music Mate works.

  • Low / High Note – choose your note range. A good note range to begin with is something moderate, such as C4 to C5. As you get better, you can make the range bigger.
  • Playback BPM – tempo when the device plays notes (Auto Play)
  • Music BPM – written tempo on the score (quarter note = value)
  • Accidental % – choose how many notes are converted to random accidentals in a Random practice mode
  • Omit notes at/above % correct – leave out notes you already know well
  • Minimum Correct Count – require enough correct plays before a note counts as learned
  • Include notes with MsAvg above this value
  • Auto start on appear – start listening automatically when the app opens
  • Turn note statistics on or off
  • Turn session statistics on or off
  • Reset – restore all settings on this, Home, and Advanced pages to defaults

Statistics Page

The Statistics page shows how you have been doing.

Note Data

  • the note name and octave number
  • how many times it was correct
  • how many times it was wrong
  • the percent correct
  • the average number of milliseconds to play correctly

Session Data

A session is completed when you play all the notes on the staff correctly, in order.

  • the date and time
  • the tune or scale used
  • the key
  • percent of notes heard that were correct
  • the note range used
  • the tempo
  • timing consistency

This helps you see your progress over time.

The data can be sorted by a long touch on the heading of any column.

Advanced Page

The Advanced page has extra settings. These are mainly for adults, teachers, or experienced users.

  • Pitch Tolerance
  • RMS Threshold – eliminates background noise
  • Cooldown
  • Pitch Offset – to correct the pitch measurement if it should be off
  • Delay Between Repeats
  • Wrong Debounce
  • Pitch Window Size
  • Smoothing Window
  • Pitch Confidence

If you are not sure what these mean, it is fine to leave them alone. There is also a Reset button to return all settings to the starting values.

Things to Know

Music Mate is always improving, but sound detection is not perfect yet.

  • a note in the wrong octave may be accepted
  • very low or very high notes may be harder to detect
  • your % correct may look a little lower than expected

We are continuing to improve the app.

Tips for Success

  • Start with a comfortable note range.
  • Practice slowly at first.
  • Use Auto Play to hear how notes should sound.
  • Watch for green boxes.
  • Keep practicing notes that are still difficult.
  • Try Random mode for extra challenge.

Random Tune – Advanced Details

Candidate Note Pool

Candidate notes are chosen for the selected scale and key signature.

For each candidate note, the code checks if we have past statistics for that note. If we do, we will skip (exclude) the note when building the random list if it is already mastered — i.e. its accuracy percentage is at or above CorrectThreshold and it has been answered correctly at least MinCorrectCount times and its average milliseconds is less than the threshold. In other words, “we’ve practiced this enough and you’re accurate, so don’t include it.”

Settings that control this behaviour:

  • LowestNote / HighestNote – the note range to consider
  • Key and SelectedScale – which notes belong to the chosen scale
  • CorrectThreshold – the accuracy percent needed to consider a note “accurate”
  • MinCorrectCount – how many correct hits are required before accuracy is trusted
  • OmitMsAvgThreshold – the average response-time cutoff (ms) below which a note is skipped

Premium users can also set Accidental % which causes some notes to be randomly modified by the addition of a sharp, flat, or natural symbol.

Ordering of the Random Notes

  • Pick a random note to begin the list.
  • Repeatedly pick the next note from the remaining notes until none are left.
  • Prefer notes that are close to the current one in the scale (one step away is most likely, two steps a bit less likely, and so on up to seven steps).
  • The code gives a much higher chance to 1-step moves, lower for 2-step, lower still for 3-step, and so on.
  • If no “close” notes remain, pick any remaining note at random.
  • Each chosen note is removed from the pool so notes don’t repeat.

Result: the sequence tends to move in small steps most of the time, with occasional bigger jumps.
