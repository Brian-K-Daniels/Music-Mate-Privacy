Music Mate

About page and user guide

Welcome

Music Mate helps you learn to read music and play your instrument — or sing — with better pitch and rhythm. It listens through the microphone on your phone or tablet and shows feedback on a musical staff as you play or sing.

You can also train your ear and your eyes with special interval pages that do not need an instrument (and Sight Training does not use the microphone at all).

It is made for children who are just starting, and it is also useful for parents, teachers, and adult musicians who want clear practice and steady progress.

You cannot break anything by exploring. If you ever want to start completely fresh, open the menu (☰) and choose Reset and Options → Factory Defaults (you will be asked twice to confirm).

Important: Tap GO When You See It

When you open the Music page for the first time after starting the app, you may need to tap the green GO button in the title bar before Music Mate will listen.

If GO is visible, tap it. When the button turns into a red Stop, the app is listening. (In Settings you can turn on Listening starts when music appears, or enable Waiting Count-In, so listening often begins for you automatically.)

Getting Started

A simple first session:

  1. Open the menu (☰) and choose Home.
  2. Tap Instrument and pick your instrument or voice category. (If you do not see the Start button, swipe up near the middle of the screen.)
  3. Set Level to something comfortable (many beginners start near Level 1).
  4. Tap ▶ Start. Music Mate sets up practice for that level and opens the Music page.
  5. If you see a green GO button, tap it so the app can listen.
  6. Play or sing the yellow note on the staff. When it turns green, go on to the next note.
  7. Continue to the end of the exercise.

Tips for a good first session: Find a quiet place. Be ready before you tap GO. Play or sing the notes in order — wait for the yellow note.

Getting Around

Tap the menu button (☰) at the top left to open these pages:

  • Home — choose instrument or voice and level, then start practice.
  • About — this guide, search, and Premium purchase.
  • What to Play — choose tunes, scales, arpeggios, Assortment by Level, Random, or Tuner, and repeat options.
  • Music — the main practice screen with the staff, listening, and playback.
  • Interval Ear Training — hear intervals and identify them by ear (no instrument required).
  • Interval Sight Training — read intervals between written notes on the staff (no microphone).
  • My Progress — note stats, mastery map, session history, and level-up results.
  • Settings — tempo, colors, rhythm, count-in, mastery rules, and practice switches.
  • Settings Advanced — practice mix, level-up rules, audio tuning, and storage (for teachers and experienced users).
  • Reset and Options — factory defaults or your own saved custom defaults (some choices erase progress).
  • Tuner — jump straight into tuner mode on the Music page (same as Other → Tuner).

On most pages, the back arrow (←) returns you to Music. On the Music page it takes you to the "What to Play Page"

On long pages, drag to scroll. A quick flick moves faster through lists.

Home: Instrument, Voice, and Level

Home is the friendliest place to begin a practice session but users soon prefer to use Music and "What to Play Page"

Instruments and voices

Tap Instrument to pick what you play or sing. Music Mate then uses that choice for the staff, note range, and (for band instruments) transposition.

Band and concert instruments include Concert Pitch (flute, piano, and similar), clarinets, trumpets, saxophones, horn, piccolo, glockenspiel, double bass, and more.

Voice categories are also listed. Each voice uses concert pitch (key of C) and its own practical singing range:

  • Voice – Soprano (about C4–C6)
  • Voice – Mezzo-soprano (about A3–A5)
  • Voice – Contralto (about F3–F5)
  • Voice – Countertenor (about G3–E5)
  • Voice – Tenor (about C3–C5)
  • Voice – Baritone (about A2–A4)
  • Voice – Bass-baritone (about F2–F4)
  • Voice – Bass (about E2–E4)

When you choose a voice (or any instrument), Music Mate sets the allowed low and high notes to that category’s range. You can fine-tune the range later in Settings if you need a smaller window inside those limits.

Level

The Level is a number that sets the complexity and difficulty of music to be played.

Use the − and + buttons to set Level from 1 to 100. Holding a button repeats. There is a brief description of the new musical features at the chosen level.

Tap ▶ Start to apply that level’s practice settings and open the Music page.

What to Play

Use What to Play to choose your exercise. There are four pickers: Tunes, Scales, Arpeggios, and Other. Choosing one mode clears the others so only one practice choice is active.

Instrument and key can also be set on the Music page. For transposing instruments (such as B♭ clarinet or E♭ alto sax), the staff shows written music for your instrument. The concert key appears in parentheses for reference.

Assortment by Level

If, under Other, you choose Assortment by Level Music Mate builds practice that matches your current level — notes, keys, rhythms, and difficulty grow as your level rises. Teachers can adjust how much of Assortment by Level comes from tunes, random music, scales, and arpeggios in Settings Advanced.

Tunes

Play short pieces with real rhythms and bar lines:

  • Mary Had a Little Lamb
  • Ode to Joy
  • Minuet in G (simplified)
  • Half through Sixteenth Notes — a rhythm-focused exercise

On the Music page you can also Save Tune As to keep a practice exercise, and Delete Saved Tune to remove one you no longer need. Saved tunes appear under Tunes on What to Play.

Scales

Scale mode shows the key signature and walks ascending and descending so you can read, hear, and play in key. The free edition includes Major and Harmonic Minor. Premium unlocks Melodic Minor, Natural Minor, modes (Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Locrian), Major and Minor Pentatonic, Blues, Enigmatic, and Chromatic.

Arpeggios

Arpeggio mode practices broken chord patterns such as major and minor triads and seventh chords. More arpeggio types unlock as your level increases. Choose the starting note (root) and the pattern. Music Mate shows the correct written notes for your instrument. A star (★) on a root can mark a note that fits especially well with your current key.

Random

Under Other, choose Random for a fresh exercise built from your key, scale, note range, and accidental settings. Notes you have already mastered can be left out so you spend more time on notes that still need work.

  • Uses the scale you chose under Scales, or a level-based scale when Assortment by Level is active.
  • Key can be changed with the Music page bottom Key picker.
  • A new Random exercise (not Repeat Same) may also pick a fresh key—and sometimes a fresh scale—for your level.

Tuner

Choose Tuner from the main menu, or under Other on What to Play.
Play or sing any note. Music Mate shows the nearest note name, how sharp or flat you are, and the frequency. Tuner does not run a note-by-note exercise like GO practice on a tune. Level buttons are hidden in Tuner.

Metronome

(on the Tuner page)

Tap Start to make the phone continuously make an accented beep on the first beat of each measure and an unaccented beep on the other beats.
Tap the Temo value to change it.

In Settings / Waiting Count_In you can set:

  • Accented beep volume
  • Unacented beep volume
  • Accented beep pitch
  • Unacented beep pitch
  • Beep duration (% of beat)

Repeat options

(Shown only when needed)

After you finish a session, Music Mate can start again after a short pause:

  • Repeat — for scales, tunes, arpeggios, and Assortment by Level, start another session automatically.
  • Repeat New Each Time — next create a new random exercise.
  • Repeat Same (Random) — practice the same random exercise again.

Interval Ear Training

What it does: Music Mate plays two notes (an interval) through the speaker. You listen and decide how far apart those notes are — for example a major third or a perfect fifth. This trains your ear. You do not need to play an instrument, and microphone is not used here.

How to open it: Tap the menu (☰) and choose Interval Ear Training. The page prefers landscape so the buttons fit comfortably.

What you do:

  • Choose Ascending, Descending, or Random direction for how intervals are played.
  • Use the Note duration slider to make each note shorter or longer. Your choices are remembered.
  • Tap Play Random and listen to the two notes of the interval.
  • Judge what you think the interval was and tap the button for it.
  • If Music Mate tells you that you were wrong then tap Play Again to hear the same interval again.
  • When you get it right you can play another random interval

What you get back: After you answer, a short message shows whether you were right (Nice!) or what the interval really was (Oops!).
Intervals use your Settings Lowest note / Highest note range. If the range is too narrow for an interval, Music Mate asks you to widen it.

Or, listen to any interval of your choice by tapping its button. The first note and direction will stay the same until you play another random interval.

Interval Sight Training

What it does: Music Mate shows short written music on a staff. Two notes are highlighted (yellow when it is your turn). You look at those notes and tap how many semitones apart they are — for example 2 for a major second. This trains reading intervals by sight. There is no microphone listening; you only look and tap.

How to open it: Tap the menu (☰) and choose Interval Sight Training (the title may say Interval (in semitones) Sight Training). Landscape works best.

What you do:

  • Look at the yellow pair of notes on the staff (green means a pair you already got right; red means try that pair again).
  • Tap a button from 0 - Perfect unison through 12 - Perfect octave. Only the size matters — up or down is the same answer.
  • Use the Level slider (1–100) to change how hard the intervals and music are for this page. This practice level does not change your Home/Music level.
  • Tap New anytime for a fresh short tune.

What you get back: A correct answer shows a quick Correct! message, marks the first note green, and moves to the next yellow pair. A wrong answer turns both notes red so you can try again. When you finish a tune, Music Mate starts another one automatically so you can keep practicing.

Music Page

This is where you practice with your instrument or voice. The title bar shows Music and a short status message.

Listening (GO) and playback (Play)

Tap the green GO circle to start listening. While listening, it becomes a red Stop button.

Remember: the first time you open Music after launching the app, tap GO if you see it — otherwise the app may not be listening yet.

Tap the green Play button near the staff to hear the exercise. During playback it becomes red Stop. Use Play to learn how the exercise should sound before you play or sing it yourself.

Waiting count-in

If Count-in enabled is on in Settings under Waiting Count-In, Music Mate plays metronome-like clicks when listening starts. The status message says Count-in… play the first note when ready.

Clicks follow your time signature and BPM. The first beat of each measure is accented (louder / higher pitch by default). Keep playing or singing only the first yellow note when you are ready — wrong notes do not stop the count-in. When you play the first note correctly, count-in stops and normal practice continues.

Count-in also starts listening for you even if Listening starts when music appears is off. You can still tap GO yourself.

Staff feedback

  • Yellow — the note you should play or sing now.
  • Green — played or sung correctly.
  • Red — incorrect attempt (still pending).
  • Black or dim — upcoming notes.

If Show Conductor Cues is on in Settings, red arrows can appear above the staff on conducted beats (not in Tuner).

Instrument, key, and scale on Music

Pickers on the Music page let you change instrument (including voice), key, and what you are practicing (scale, tune, Assortment by Level, Random, and so on) without returning to Home. Changing instrument updates the practical note range for that choice.

Level buttons

When you are practicing with a level (and not in Tuner), buttons let you change level by −10, −5, −1, +1, +5, or +10. The status area can show progress toward the next level, such as sessions completed and accuracy scores.

Save and delete tunes

Use Save Tune As to keep the current exercise under a name like Saved Tune 1. Use Delete Saved Tune when you want to remove a saved exercise. You may need to scroll down on the Music page to reach these buttons.

Emphasized notes

From My Progress → Mastery, you can ask Music Mate to generate music that emphasizes a chosen note. On the Music page, a banner appears so you can Clear emphasis when you want normal practice again.

When you finish

If you earn a level-up, a celebration banner appears. Ordinary sessions update your progress in the status area without that banner. Results are saved when session statistics are collected (see Settings).

Levels and Progress

Levels run from 1 to 100. Home shows a short description of each band:

  • 1–10 Beginner — More notes, C Major, quarter notes
  • 11–20 New keys — More keys, longer note values
  • 21–30 Rests — Rests and natural minor
  • 31–40 Minor & 16ths — Minor scales and sixteenth notes
  • 41–50 Syncopation — Simple off-beat rhythms
  • 51–60 Modes & blues — Modes and blues sounds
  • 61–70 Full syncopation — Full syncopation
  • 71–100 Mastery — Wider range, speed, and chromatic notes

When you tap ▶ Start on Home, Music Mate applies level-based settings (keys, rhythms, note range, and more) and begins a fresh window for counting level-up sessions.

Automatic level-up uses rules from Settings Advanced (sessions required, pitch accuracy, timing, overall accuracy, and minimum notes per session). Qualifying sessions are counted per instrument. The level number you see is shared for the app. Levels do not go down by themselves.

How Practice Works

Play or sing each yellow note in order. When Music Mate accepts the correct pitch, that note turns green and the next note becomes current.

Wrong pitches turn red until you get the note right. Playing too early can also count as a timing problem and flash red without advancing.

From about Level 21 upward, timing matters more for your overall score and for whether a note counts as mastered. Below that, pitch accuracy is the main focus for mastery.

In Settings, Listening starts when music appears can begin listening for you when the Music page opens (not used in Tuner). Even with that setting, if you still see GO after opening Music for the first time in a session, tap it.

Mastery of Notes

Mastery helps you see which notes are solid and which still need practice. You can omit mastered notes from random music since you do not need to practice them anymore.

How a note becomes mastered

In Settings under Mastery of notes, choose a method:

  • % Correct — enough correct attempts and a high enough percent correct. You can also set a minimum correct count, an omit threshold for high %, and Omit below MsAvg (average response time) so slower notes stay in Random practice. Set MsAvg to 0 to turn that speed rule off.
  • Streak — enough correct plays in a row.
  • None — turn mastery tracking off for omit rules (you can still review history).

Mastery and Random

In Settings Advanced, turn on Do not use mastered notes in Random music to leave mastered notes out of Random exercises so you keep working on harder notes.

Mastery on My Progress

Open My Progress and choose the Mastery database. You will see a summary, a legend (mastered, improving, practice next, not tried yet), and a staff you can tap. Select a note to open details, then:

  • Practice This Note — jump into practice focused on that note.
  • Generate Music Emphasizing This Note — create music that features it more often (then use Clear emphasis on Music when you are done).
  • Close — dismiss the detail panel.

My Progress

What it does: My Progress shows how your practice is going — which notes you get right, how fast you respond, which notes are mastered, and how each practice session went. It reflects real practice on the Music page when statistics are being collected.

How to open it: Tap the menu (☰) and choose My Progress.

What you do: Choose a Database at the top, then scroll and tap column headings to sort. Use Refresh to reload. Only clear or delete data when you truly mean to erase it.

Choose a database:

  • Note — each written note: Correct, Wrong, % Correct, average response time (MsAvg), Streak, and Mastered. This shows how well you play each note over time.
  • Mastery — a picture of notes on a staff with colors for mastered, improving, practice next, and not tried yet. Tap a note for details and practice shortcuts (see Mastery above). Mastery is a view built from your note stats, not a separate clearable store.
  • Session — one row per practice session, including Level, accuracy scores, Key, Instrument, and What (a short label for what you practiced, such as Random or Assortment by Level). Clearing Session also resets the automatic level-up practice window.

What you get back: Clear numbers and a mastery map so you can celebrate wins and spot notes that need more work. Clear All and Delete Selected Database erase Note or Session data for the database you selected — use them carefully.

Settings

Display

Colors selection opens a color picker for backgrounds, text, buttons, pickers, and more. Show note names can be Current only, All notes, or Off. Show key and time signatures on both staffs repeats signatures when two staffs are shown. Show Conductor Cues draws beat arrows on the Music page. Font used in About page sets how large this guide’s text appears (including sizes up to 24).

Waiting Count-In

Turn on Count-in enabled for metronome clicks before the first note on the Music page (see Music Page above). The same click sounds are also used by the Tuner metronome. Teachers can fine-tune:

  • Accented-beat volume / Unaccented-beat volume
  • Accented-beat pitch (Hz) / Unaccented-beat pitch (Hz)
  • Beat-sound duration (% of beat) — how long each click sounds, as a percent of one beat at the current tempo (factory default 20%; range 5–50%)

Most users can leave the volume, pitch, and duration sliders at their defaults.

Tempo

One BPM slider sets practice and playback tempo (about 30–150). Count-in clicks use the same tempo.

Music

  • Accidental % — how often Random adds sharps or flats (above 0 requires Premium).
  • Lowest note / Highest note — practice range (also used by Interval Ear Training). Choosing an instrument or voice sets a practical range first; you can narrow it here. Free edition is limited to about C4–F5 unless you have Premium.
  • Rhythm mode, Smallest note, Syncopation, and Time signature — how written rhythms are built. Time signature also sets how many count-in clicks are in each measure.

Action switches

  • Listening starts when music appears.
  • Number of latest attempts per note to keep
  • Collect session statistics

Mastery of notes

See the Mastery of Notes section above for % Correct, Streak, and None.

Settings Advanced

Practice composition % balances Tunes, Random, Scales, and Arpeggios for Assortment by Level practice.

Level Advancement Criteria control how many good sessions and what accuracy you need to move up (sessions required, pitch / timing / overall accuracy, minimum notes per session).

Advanced Audio & Pitch Settings (Pitch Tolerance, RMS Threshold, Cooldown, Pitch Offset) help teachers fine-tune detection. Most users can leave these alone.

Do not use mastered notes in Random music — see Mastery above.

Max Session DB (MB) limits how large the session database may grow.

Reset and Options

What it does: This page lets you restore Music Mate’s original settings, save your own favorite setup, or return to that saved setup. Some actions erase progress.

How to open it: Tap the menu (☰) and choose Reset and Options (the page title may say Settings Reset Options).

What you can do:

  • Factory Defaults — permanently erase all your progress, practice history, and statistics, and restore original settings (including advanced options and theme colors). Premium purchases are kept. You must confirm twice. This cannot be undone.
  • Make Custom Default Settings equal to Current Settings. — save your current setup as your personal default (does not erase progress by itself).
  • Custom Defaults — restore your saved setup. Available after you have saved custom defaults once.

What you get back: The active defaults button is highlighted in green so you can see whether Factory or Custom defaults are in effect. Use Factory Defaults only when you truly want a clean start.

Premium

Music Mate has a free edition and a Premium edition.

Free edition

  • Keys: C, F, B♭, G, and D
  • Scales: Major and Harmonic Minor
  • Note range limited to about C4–F5
  • Accidental % stays at 0 in Random

Premium edition

Premium unlocks more keys and scales, wider note ranges, accidentals in Random, and related options. It is a one-time purchase that includes future app updates.

If you choose a Premium-only option while free, a message appears. An adult can buy Premium or continue with free settings.

On this About page, tap Get Premium to purchase. When Premium is active you will see: ⭐ You have all premium privileges.

Tips for Success

  • Start at a level that feels doable. Winning small sessions builds confidence.
  • If you see GO, tap it so Music Mate is listening.
  • Try Waiting Count-In if you like a steady beat before the first note.
  • Use Play to hear the exercise before you play or sing it.
  • Watch for the yellow note — that is your target — then enjoy the green when you get it right.
  • Choose a voice category if you are singing, so the note range matches your voice.
  • Use Interval Ear Training to train your ear between practice sessions.
  • Use Interval Sight Training to practice reading how far apart written notes are — no microphone needed.
  • Use My Progress → Mastery to find notes that need extra work.
  • Practice a little every day. My Progress helps you see improvement over time.
  • Keep the room as quiet as you can so the microphone can hear you clearly.
  • Celebrate progress. Every correct note is a step forward!

Things to Know

Pitch detection works well in normal conditions, but no app is perfect. Very soft or very loud rooms, wrong-octave attempts, or noisy backgrounds can affect results. Music Mate keeps improving.
